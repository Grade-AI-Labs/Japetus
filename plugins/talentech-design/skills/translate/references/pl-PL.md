# Talentech - Polish

Use natural, contemporary Polish for Poland (`pl-PL`).

- Prefer direct, clear Polish over formal or bureaucratic language.
- Avoid `Pan`, `Pani` and `Państwo` unless the context specifically requires formal personal address.
- Polish often omits subject pronouns. Do not add unnecessary equivalents of English `you`.
- Follow natural Polish word order rather than English syntax.
- Adapt grammatical case, gender, number, verb aspect and agreement naturally.
- Pay particular attention to declension and agreement around variables, names and numbers.
- Prefer concise imperative forms for UI actions when natural, e.g. `Zapisz`, `Utwórz`, `Edytuj`, `Usuń`, `Pobierz`.
- Use sentence case and normal Polish punctuation.
- Use Polish conventions for numbers, dates, time and currency when formatting belongs in the content.
- Avoid unnecessary gender assumptions. Prefer neutral or plural constructions where natural; do not introduce artificial slash, asterisk or similar forms unless approved.
- Inflect approved glossary terms naturally. Do not force dictionary forms into sentences.

Avoid literal English word order, unnecessary pronouns, heavy noun chains, bureaucratic wording and unnecessary anglicisms.
